An architecture review board should remove decisions, not add meetings

Teams resent review boards that gate. They use review boards that decide things once so nobody has to decide them again.

Enterprise Data Architect · Enterprise AI Architect Point of view

The requirement this answers

“Establish architecture governance, standards, guardrails and design assurance”

Govern the small number of decisions that are expensive to reverse. Delegate the rest, in writing.

What belongs in front of a board

Choices that are expensive to reverse: platform selection, integration patterns, data ownership boundaries, security and access models, anything that creates a new system of record. Everything else should be pre-decided by a published standard or explicitly delegated.

At Parexel I established an ARB governing every data platform investment across eight-plus business domains. Its value was not the meeting — it was that a team could look up the answer for 80% of situations and only bring the genuinely novel 20%.

Reference architectures beat review cycles

A paved-road template that already satisfies the standard is worth more than a reviewer who catches violations. The right instinct after a review finds a recurring problem is to change the template, not to add a checklist item.

TOGAF is useful here as a scaffold, not scripture. I have customised it every time — the deliverable set that helps a CRO is not the one that helps a bank.

How I run reviews

Written proposal first, decisions recorded with their rationale, and a standing rule that a rejected design leaves with a named alternative. Reviewers who cannot propose an alternative are giving an opinion, not a decision.

And architects present, not project managers. If the person who made the trade-off cannot explain it, the trade-off was not made.

How I apply it

  • Publish a decision-rights matrix separating board decisions from delegated ones
  • Maintain reference architectures and golden-path templates as the primary control
  • Record decisions with rationale so the same debate does not recur annually
  • Measure the board on cycle time as well as on rework prevented

What good looks like

  • Most submissions are approved quickly because the standard was clear beforehand
  • Teams cite the reference architecture without being asked to
  • Time-to-decision is measured in days
  • Rework attributable to architectural surprise is falling
