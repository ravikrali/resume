Half your data engineering capacity is defensive. That is the opportunity.

Roughly 53% of data engineering capacity goes to maintaining and troubleshooting what already exists, with schema drift the single largest category. This is a capacity reallocation, not a cost-cutting exercise.

Enterprise Data Architect · Director, AI & Data Management Point of view

The requirement this answers

“Modernise the enterprise data platform and data engineering function”

Pipelines you specify, behaviour that adapts, context as the deliverable — and the trust plane built first.

Six inversions

Pipelines you write become pipelines you specify. Rules anticipated in advance become behaviour learned and adapted. Tables as the deliverable become context as the deliverable. Structured-only becomes multimodal by default. Batch-anchored freshness becomes continuous by default. Humans in every loop become humans on the loop.

None of this is speculative any more — Databricks Lakeflow, Snowflake smart pipelines and Fabric's agent-authorable Data Factory all shipped agentic capability at GA in 2026.

The highest-return change available

Treat schema as observed state — versioned and diffed — rather than pinned at build time. Drift gets classified automatically as additive, renamed or semantic, and an agent proposes the mapping with a confidence score. Schema drift moves from "breaks the pipeline at 3am" to "raises a reviewable proposal."

That one change addresses the largest single category of the maintenance tax.

The failure mode to avoid

The dbt State of Analytics Engineering 2026 data is blunt: 72% prioritise AI-assisted coding, 24% prioritise AI-assisted pipeline management, and 71% name incorrect outputs reaching stakeholders as a top concern. Generating pipelines four times faster without matching testing and observability generates defects four times faster too.

Sequence the trust plane — lineage, observability, learned quality monitors, eval harnesses, audit — before the authoring agents. It is the least popular recommendation I make and the one that matters most.

How I apply it

  • Phase 0 is measurement: harvest metadata, build the real dependency graph, quantify the maintenance tax
  • One lighthouse domain end to end, proven at numerical parity across a full business cycle including period-end
  • Declarative pipelines with contract enforcement — breaking changes fail at build
  • Cost attributed per pipeline, per data product, per consumer

What good looks like

  • Share of capacity spent firefighting is a tracked, falling number
  • Freshness for decision-driving domains measured in minutes, not the nightly window
  • Column-level lineage covers a growing share of the estate
  • Legacy platforms actually get switched off
