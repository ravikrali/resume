Traditional MDM is now the bottleneck between you and agentic AI

MDM hubs were optimised for a 2010 enterprise: rules authored upfront, batch reconciliation overnight, stewards clearing queues. Every one of those assumptions breaks when agents are the consumer.

Director, AI & Data Management · Enterprise Data Architect Point of view

The requirement this answers

“Lead Master Data Management strategy, platform selection and implementation”

Models propose truth, events settle it, and stewards adjudicate policy — not transactions.

Three shifts

From rules to models: humans encoding match and survivorship logic upfront gives way to semantic matching and learned, attribute-level survivorship. Traditional fuzzy matching scores around 37% F1 on entity-resolution benchmarks; an LLM adjudicating the ambiguous band gets past 95% precision.

From batch to events: nightly reconciliation means the golden record is always at least 24 hours stale. Event-driven settlement means it is current by construction.

From queues to judgement: stewards move from unbounded task lists to a curated set of high-value exceptions, with the system learning from each decision.

The economics change direction

Traditional MDM has an ascending cost curve — per-master-row licensing punishes you for mastering more data, and steward headcount scales with volume. AI-native MDM front-loads engineering investment and then declines as models absorb stewardship volume. Crossover typically lands in year two; by year three the annual cost runs 30–40% below the traditional trajectory.

Every Gartner Leader still prices on volume. That is worth knowing before you sign a three-year renewal.

How to get there without a rewrite

Strangler fig, over roughly 18 months. Diagnose and baseline against ground truth. Run shadow resolution in parallel at zero production risk. Put one bounded domain into production with conservative autonomy thresholds. Expand domain by domain behind an anti-corruption layer. Then activate AI-first consumption and decommission.

Do not port the legacy rules. Use them as test cases. And set the legacy exit date in phase two — permanent hybrids are the most expensive outcome available.

How I apply it

  • Establish ground truth and measure the incumbent before proposing a replacement
  • Four-stage resolution: embedding blocking, fast classifier, LLM adjudication, steward exception
  • Migrate against the renewal calendar for contractual leverage
  • Commit a decommission date with an accountable executive

What good looks like

  • Match precision and recall are measured against ground truth, not asserted
  • Stewards can explain a merge to a compliance officer in plain language
  • Golden records are exposed as a real-time API and event stream, not a nightly extract
  • Unit cost per mastered record is falling
