AI strategy is a portfolio decision, not a technology decision

Most AI strategies fail at problem selection, not at model selection. The work is choosing which expensive, repetitive workflows deserve funding — and being willing to say no to the rest.

Enterprise AI Architect · Director, AI & Data Management Point of view

The requirement this answers

“Define and drive the enterprise AI strategy and roadmap”

Start from a costly workflow, not from a capability you want to use.

The failure mode

Programmes that begin with "we should use AI" behave differently from programmes that begin with "this process costs us £4m a year and three people hate it." The second kind survives its first bad demo. The first kind becomes AI theatre — visible, well-attended, and structurally unable to prove it was worth anything.

Roughly 88% of organisations now use AI somewhere. Only about a quarter of initiatives hit their expected return. The gap is almost never the model.

What I actually do

I run AI strategy as six repeating moves: problem selection, platform fit, direction (customer-facing versus internal), governance and risk, adoption, and measurement. Each has a gate. A use case that cannot name its baseline metric before build does not get funded.

Platform fit matters more than people expect. Generative, agentic and classical ML solve genuinely different problems. Forecasting and demand planning are not LLM problems. Multi-step transactional work is not a RAG problem. Getting this wrong is how you end up in the 40% of agentic projects Gartner expects to be cancelled by 2027.

The uncomfortable part

Internal-facing AI is usually the better first bet and always the less glamorous one. It is a lower-stakes environment to build organisational fluency, and it is reversible. Customer-facing AI is visible, which means its failures are also visible and hard to walk back.

How I apply it

  • Inventory workflows by cost and repetition before discussing technology at all
  • Assign each candidate a platform class — generative, agentic, or classical ML — and justify it
  • Run two tracks with separate measurement: outward-facing and inward-facing
  • Gate funding on a small, fixed KPI set agreed before the first sprint

What good looks like

  • Every funded use case has a named business owner and a measured pre-build baseline
  • At least one use case has been killed at a gate — a portfolio with no failures is not being measured
  • Run cost is budgeted alongside build cost
  • Leadership can say what the AI portfolio is for in one sentence
