Most data programmes fail on ownership, not on technology

Unclear ownership, absent leadership direction and poor upstream requirements outrank technical debt as constraints. If nobody owns customer data, no architecture fixes it.

Director, AI & Data Management · Enterprise Data Architect Point of view

The requirement this answers

“Define the target operating model for data and AI across the enterprise”

Ownership, decision rights and decommission accountability are Phase 0 deliverables, not change-management afterthoughts.

The question I ask first

Who is accountable — by name, not by function — for the quality of this domain's data? In most organisations the honest answer is nobody, or a committee, which is the same answer. Everything downstream of that gap is expensive.

The second question: what gets switched off when this is delivered? Programmes without a decommission commitment produce permanent hybrids, two cost lines and two versions of the truth.

Structure follows consumption

Whether you land on mesh, fabric, a central platform team or a federated model matters far less than whether the boundaries follow how the business actually consumes data. Domain ownership works where domains are real and have leaders with budget. It fails where "domain" is a slide.

Run the platform as a product: named owners, published SLAs, unit economics, and a governance cadence for cost, quality and evals matching the rigour of financial close.

Roles shift, and you have to say so

Data engineers move from pipeline authors to platform and context owners. Stewards move from clearing queues to adjudicating ambiguity. That is a genuine elevation, but only if it is framed and resourced as one. Framed as efficiency, it reads as a threat and gets resisted — and roughly a third of workers already admit to quietly resisting AI tools.

Adoption is a people problem. A working prototype nobody uses returns exactly zero.

How I apply it

  • Name accountable owners per domain before designing anything
  • Publish decision rights across business, platform and governance
  • Commit decommission dates with an accountable executive attached
  • Fund role redefinition and skills uplift as a workstream, not as a footnote

What good looks like

  • Every critical domain has a named owner who would be called first
  • The platform publishes SLAs and its own unit economics
  • Legacy systems are actually retired on the dates committed
  • Adoption is measured, and low adoption triggers a response
