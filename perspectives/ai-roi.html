If you cannot measure it, you are not investing — you are hoping

79% of organisations report productivity gains from AI. About 29% can confidently measure the return. That gap is where most programmes quietly die.

Director, AI & Data Management · Enterprise AI Architect Point of view

The requirement this answers

“Own the business case and demonstrate measurable value from AI investment”

Set the baseline before you build, budget the run cost, and re-measure at every stage.

Why AI ROI is genuinely hard

It is not simply that finance teams are being difficult. AI value is diffuse — spread across efficiency, decision quality, error reduction and risk prevention simultaneously, which single-metric ROI models cannot capture. It compounds slowly, with organisations now expecting satisfactory return in two to four years rather than the seven-to-twelve-month payback typical of conventional IT.

And a great deal of the value is prevention. Anomaly detection creates value by stopping things happening, which is structurally harder to put in a spreadsheet than new revenue.

The investment ladder

I right-size spend against the problem using six rungs: use features already in your existing software; call a frontier model by API; run RAG over your proprietary data; self-host a smaller model; fine-tune; build a foundation model. Best returns concentrate overwhelmingly in the first three rungs. Custom foundation models are almost never justified outside a handful of organisations.

Most enterprises are still, correctly, in buy-and-integrate territory. The discipline is refusing to climb the ladder for reasons of prestige.

What I insist on

Separate hard ROI from soft ROI and track both, but be honest that only hard ROI appears in the P&L. Budget monitoring, retraining, governance and human review as ongoing cost — unbudgeted run cost is one of the most common reasons finance leaders say they cannot demonstrate AI return.

How I apply it

  • Define the metric and measure the baseline before a line of code is written
  • Model total cost of ownership including evals, human review and retraining
  • Re-measure at experimentation, integration and scale — not just at launch
  • Track payback period, adoption rate and quality as three separate gates

What good looks like

  • Payback modelled per use case, not for "AI" as a category
  • Weekly active usage above 60% for tools claimed to be adopted
  • Cost-per-outcome tracked against the human baseline it replaced
  • Someone can name what was decommissioned because of the new system
