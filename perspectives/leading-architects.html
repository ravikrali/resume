Developing architects and technical leads

The measure of a senior architecture role is what the organization can decide well without you in the room.

Director, AI & Data Management · Enterprise AI Architect Point of view

The requirement this answers

“Lead, mentor and grow a team of architects and technical leads”

Teach the pattern and the trade-off so the team can reach the answer independently.

What I have run

Teams of 10 to 15 architects, data engineers, and solution leads at KPMG with full P&L, plus hiring, workforce planning, interviewing, onboarding, succession planning, performance management, and career development. Earlier, a regional BI and data warehousing practice at TCS with responsibility for recruiting, training, mentoring, and pipeline.

At Parexel the shape changed to executive-level coaching for senior architects and technical leads on solution quality, stakeholder engagement, and enterprise alignment, along with ownership of architecture capability maturity and long-term talent strategy.

How I coach

Design decisions are made in the open with the reasoning visible. A junior architect who watches three options weighed learns more than one handed the conclusion. When I review a design I ask what was considered and rejected, and if there is no answer, that is the coaching conversation.

Stakeholder engagement is a teachable skill that technical leads are rarely taught. Bringing a senior engineer into an executive workshop and debriefing afterwards on what landed has produced more development than formal training.

The administrative half

Performance management done properly, career paths that do not require becoming a manager, succession planning ahead of a resignation, and giving people direct feedback early. Global onshore and offshore teams add a further requirement to design deliberately for time zones rather than relying on goodwill.

How I apply it

  • Bring senior technical staff into executive conversations, then debrief
  • Review the alternatives considered, not only the design chosen
  • Maintain a technical career track that does not require moving into management
  • Plan succession for critical roles before it becomes urgent

What good looks like

  • Designs improve before they reach you
  • People are promoted from within the team
  • Architects can defend a trade-off to an executive without you present
  • Retention is strong among the people you most want to keep
