The job is building architects, not being the architect

A senior architect who makes every decision creates a team that cannot make any. The measure of the role is what the organisation can decide well without you in the room.

Director, AI & Data Management · Enterprise AI Architect Point of view

The requirement this answers

“Lead, mentor and grow a team of architects and technical leads”

Teach the pattern and the trade-off, not the answer.

What I have actually run

Teams of 10–15 architects, data engineers and solution leads at KPMG with full P&L, plus hiring, workforce planning, interviewing, onboarding, succession planning, performance management and career development. Before that, a regional BI and data warehousing practice at TCS with responsibility for recruiting, training, mentoring and the pipeline.

At Parexel the shape changed — executive-level coaching for senior architects and technical leads on solution quality, stakeholder engagement and enterprise alignment, plus ownership of architecture capability maturity and long-term talent strategy.

How I coach

Design decisions get made in the open, with the reasoning visible. A junior architect who watches you weigh three options and pick one learns more than one handed the conclusion. When I review a design I ask what was considered and rejected — if the answer is nothing, that is the coaching conversation.

Stakeholder engagement is a teachable skill and most technical leads are never taught it. Sitting a senior engineer next to me in an executive workshop, then debriefing what landed and what did not, has produced more growth than any training course I have sent people on.

The unglamorous half

Performance management done properly, career paths that do not require becoming a manager, succession planning before someone resigns, and telling people the truth early. Global onshore and offshore teams add a layer — you have to design for time zones deliberately rather than expecting goodwill to absorb it.

How I apply it

  • Pair senior technical staff into executive conversations, then debrief
  • Review the alternatives considered, not just the design chosen
  • Maintain a technical career track that does not force people into management
  • Plan succession for every critical role before it becomes urgent

What good looks like

  • Designs get better before they reach you
  • People are promoted from within the team
  • Architects can defend a trade-off to an executive without you present
  • Attrition is low among the people you most want to keep
